## 2026-09-01 — vet visit morning
**Logged:** 2026-09-01 20:41 UTC
**Kind:** incident
**Tags:** #morning #aggression #vet #kept-distance #texted

### What happened
Vet visit day. He had work and was not going. The morning activity — the one
that had been defusing the wake-ups — did not run.

She woke up angry, immediately.

He asked: "Hey, can we talk?"
She said: "I only have five minutes."
Then she started shouting.
She told him to go fuck himself, or fuck you — he isn't sure of the exact
wording. He describes her as very aggressive.

He asked if there was anything he could do to help. She named something; he
did it. She stayed aggressive.

He kept his distance. She left for the vet without saying goodbye, without
anything.

### What he did
- Opened with a request to talk, early, before it escalated.
- Asked what would help, then actually delivered the help.
- Kept his distance once it was clear nothing was landing.
- **Did not call her.** Chose a text instead.
- Texted, approximately: *"Hey, I'm here — text me when you can, or if you'd
  like to talk."* (He doesn't remember the exact wording.)

### What she said / did
Shouting. "Go fuck yourself" / "fuck you," wording uncertain. Sustained
aggression after he provided the help she asked for. Left without a goodbye.
Then replied to his text.

**Resolved 2026-09-01 20:51 UTC** — his text and her reply are recorded in
the next entry. The text was closer to: *"Hey, I don't want to mess things up
more than they're messy. Please call me or text me when you can so that I can
check up on you. Nothing else, so that we can discuss things. I'm here. I
care."* Her reply was "fuck you," and continued cursing.

### Read
**On the morning itself.** He handled it about as well as it can be handled.
Asking, offering, doing the thing, then stepping back and leaving a
low-pressure door open instead of chasing with a call — that's the correct
sequence, and it worked in the narrow sense that she replied. The record
should say that clearly, because he isn't going to remember it that way in a
week.

**On the aggression.** Logged as observed, not interpreted. She said it after
he asked to talk and while she was heading into a vet appointment alone, and
she stayed hostile even after getting what she asked for. That last part is
the bit that doesn't fit "she was just stressed," and it's the reason it's now
in `patterns.md` under **Watching**. One instance is one instance. If it
recurs, it isn't an incident any more.

**On the check-in.** This one is his. He swapped a visible practice for an
invisible one and, from where she was standing, the effort disappeared. Good
memories are the *result*; the check-in was the *evidence*. She could count
the check-ins. She can't count presence.

**On "we're growing apart."** It arrived the day before, and the day of, a
thing of hers he wasn't attending. That doesn't make it untrue — it makes it
about a specific absence with a date on it, not a slow drift. Those are
different problems with different fixes.

## 2026-09-01 — the rest of the day: the misread, the calls, her parents' house
**Logged:** 2026-09-01 20:51 UTC
**Kind:** incident
**Tags:** #escalation #misread #panic-attack #pursuit #left-home #aggression

### What happened
Continues directly from the morning entry. Quotes are his recall, close but
not exact unless noted.

**The text thread, after she left for the vet:**

1. **Him:** "Hey, I don't want to mess things up more than they're messy.
   Please call me or text me when you can so that I can check up on you.
   Nothing else, so that we can discuss things. I'm here. I care."
2. **Her:** "Fuck you." Then continued cursing.
3. **Him:** "I'm really sorry, but I cannot stay in conversations like that.
   I'm really here for you when things are calmer."
4. **Her:** "I won't talk again."
5. **Him:** "I understand that this is how you feel right now, and I won't
   fight to change that right now. I want to know more and understand deeper."

He then deliberately stopped calling, because of how she was speaking to him.

**The call, later:**

6. He called to ask whether she'd finished at the vet.
7. **Her:** she'd been having a **panic attack**, and he didn't call. He only
   texted. He was mean / shitty.
8. **Her:** "I said I won't talk to you again. You said you won't fight it, so
   don't fucking talk to me."
9. He tried to correct it: he'd said he wouldn't fight *to change how she
   feels*. **Her:** "No — you said 'fight to change that.' I said I won't talk
   to you again, and you said 'I won't fight that.'"

What he actually meant by (5) was that he didn't want to *fight*, because she
had just cursed at him. What she heard was that he had accepted her never
speaking to him again.

**She then decided to stay at her parents' house.**

10. He called repeatedly to talk. She mostly didn't pick up. He kept calling.
    The occasional pickup went badly and ended.
11. **Him (text):** "Come back home, I want— let's talk." Then, repeatedly,
    "please come back home."
12. **Her:** "I'm not coming back."
13. **Him:** "What the fuck is going on? I'm trying to call you all day. I feel
    so bad about the way you've been treating me and rejecting me — and now
    I'm the one saying come back."
14. **Her:** "You weren't able to, so it's not happening." And: **"Leave the
    house, then I'll come."**
15. **Her:** "Now you know why it's like that, you delusional fuck."
16. **Him:** "I understand, but as before, we've managed to turn it around.
    How we navigate those situations is everything."
17. **Her:** "Stop being so sarcastic."
18. **Him:** "I'm serious."
19. **Her:** "We also did manage." (context unclear)
20. **Him:** "That doesn't make us failures or give us an excuse to treat each
    other badly. Mistakes and misunderstandings happen. I care to understand
    and know so they stop happening."
21. **Her:** "Lullaby."
22. **Him:** "I admit I fuck up. I'm not the best speaker. I take
    accountability for my part in this."
23. He was doing the laundry. Called again. Called again.
24. **She called back — he missed it.**
25. He explained he'd been doing the laundry, and told her he missed her.

### What he did
- Held a boundary once (3), clearly and without heat.
- Stopped calling after she cursed — a deliberate choice, not neglect.
- Called later to check whether she'd finished at the vet.
- Then switched to **sustained pursuit**: repeated calls through the day,
  repeated "please come back home" texts.
- Matched her register once, at (13).
- Took accountability twice (20, 22) without her having conceded anything.
- **Deleted her abusive messages** as they came in, because he didn't want to
  read them.
- Told her he missed her, at the end.

### What she said / did
Sustained cursing across the day. "Fuck you." "Don't fucking talk to me."
"You delusional fuck." "Lullaby." Declared she wouldn't talk to him again;
went to her parents'; refused to come home; made her return conditional on
him leaving the house. Reported a panic attack at the vet and named his not
calling as the injury. Called him back at the end of the day.

### Read

**The hinge is message (5).** "I won't fight to change that right now" is the
sentence the rest of the day hangs on. He meant *I won't argue with your
feelings.* She heard *I accept that we're done talking.* Everything after
(8) is her reacting to a thing he never said. Of all the wreckage today, this
is the one piece that is cleanly, factually fixable — and he has not yet
corrected it in plain language.

**The panic attack is the wound, and it's a real collision.** She was alone at
the vet, in a panic attack, and wanted a voice. He had just been cursed at and
had made a reasonable decision not to be shouted at. Both of those are true at
the same time. But from inside a panic attack, a text is not a smaller version
of a call — it's an absence. His boundary at (3) was legitimate *and* it
landed on her at the worst possible minute. That is worth him owning without
defending the reasoning behind it.

**He inverted the thing that worked.** This morning: distance, one low-pressure
door left open, and she replied. This afternoon: repeated calls, repeated
"come back home," no space at all. The record now shows both, six hours
apart, with opposite results. See `patterns.md` P4.

**On (13).** He matched her register once. It is the only message today that
he'd want back. Noting it once, without a lecture — the notable thing is that
it happened after nine hours of absorbing, not that it happened at all.

**On the deleting.** Understandable, and it has a cost: he has now twice come
to this record unable to say what was said to him. The journal exists so the
messages don't have to live on his phone. Screenshot into `journal/`, or tell
me, and then delete.

**On what's being said to him.** This is no longer a single incident. Across
one day: "fuck you," sustained cursing, "don't fucking talk to me," "you
delusional fuck," "lullaby," a conditional demand that he leave the house.
Delivered before, during, and after he offered help, apologised, and took
accountability. This entry is why the aggression item is moving out of
**Watching** in `patterns.md` and into a named pattern (P5). No label is
being put on her and no verdict on the relationship — the record simply now
has enough dated instances to stop calling it a one-off.
